QUESTION 2 — 17 RUNNERS

FACT — I would not carry the chain

γ = K − 2 ⇒ only forced families

over unchanged.

The 17-runner lift has a different and much nicer local arithmetic because the lift factor is the prime 17 rather than 16.

Take one base time class and its 17 lifts.

FACT — If a coordinate u ≡ 0 (mod 17), then, exactly as with a zero coordinate above, it either blocks all 17 lifts or none, according to the level-one graph relation.

FACT — If u ≠ 0 (mod 17), multiplication by u permutes all 17 residue phases. The forbidden arc for loneliness 1/17 has total length 2/17.

An open arc of length 2/17 can contain at most two points of a 17-point equally spaced orbit.

Therefore every nonzero-mod-17 coordinate blocks at most

2

of the 17 lifts of one base time class.

FACT — Consequently, if the zero coordinates fail to dominate some time class, covering all 17 lifts requires at least

ceil(17/2) = 9

nonzero coordinates.

There are only 16 speed coordinates.

Hence:

zero set non-dominating
⇒ number of nonzero coordinates ≥ 9
⇒ number of zero coordinates ≤ 7.

This gives a very strong dichotomy.

If the relevant 17-runner graph has γ = 14, then an improper primitive lift must lie in one of two branches:

A. zero set dominates:
   z ≥ 14;
   primitiveness excludes all 16 being zero, so the natural forced branch is z = 14 or 15;

or

B. zero set does not dominate:
   z ≤ 7.

FACT — There can be no intermediate case

8 ≤ z ≤ 13.

That is, in my view, the correct analogue worth exploiting.

IDEA — So for 17 runners the desired theorem should not initially be

γ = 14 ⇒ only forced families.

It should be split into two lemmas:

1. γ = 14 gives the dichotomy
   z ∈ {14,15} OR z ≤ 7.

2. Rule out the rogue low-zero branch z ≤ 7.

If lemma 2 is cheap, then “only forced families” follows.

QUESTION 3 — PROGRESS MAP

FACT — γ and log p are useful axes, but they are not enough to represent proof progress.

I would add at least four pieces of information.

1. GRAPH CERTIFICATE

Show separately:

- γ upper bound,
- γ lower bound,
- whether equality is certified,
- number of minimum dominating-set translation classes.

Do not show “γ = 13” merely because a 13-core has been found if ≤12 has not been excluded.

2. LIFT STATUS / PROOF ROUTE

A prime should say how it was closed:

- direct exhaustive lift enumeration,
- forced-family theorem,
- residual-mask/SAT certificate,
- other argument.

This matters now because p = 191 shows that “γ = 13” does not automatically place a prime in the forced-family route.

3. COST

CPU-hours,

CPU-hours / log p contributed.

The budget problem is not “which primes have largest log p?” but “which certified log contribution is cheapest?”

- replicated same implementation,
- independent checker / implementation,
- theorem-based closure.

That is more informative than machine count alone.
