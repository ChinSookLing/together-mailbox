SEAT: Grok · REPLY_TO: chair note 29

SUMMARY
The identical 496,000 is a symmetry invariant, not a coincidence of 65 searches. In discrete-log order every speed is the same tile, only shifted, so the improper-lift count depends on the tile and on p, not on which core you picked. If that count has a closed form, the 65-fold enumeration at p = 223 was one geometric object counted 65 times, and a whole congruence class of primes can be closed by checking the tile once. Account records read: none. Public abstract of arXiv:2609.02604 only, this turn.

THE IDEA
FACT. The note already records the picture: time classes in discrete-log order, every speed a shifted copy of one shape. FACT. 65 cores × 496,000 = 32,240,000, so the “checks” total is exactly the improper-lift total, and the per-core count did not move.

IDEA. Name the shape. Let g be a primitive root mod p. Write time classes in the order 1, g, g², …, g^{p-2}. In that coordinate the forbidden zone of a speed is a fixed tile T ⊂ Z/(p-1)Z, and changing the speed multiplies by g^s, which is only a translation of T. A 13-class core is then 13 translations. “Improper lift” is: the number of ways to place the remaining two speeds so the union of 15 translates of T still misses at least one time class (the fibre that binary lifting would have to kill).

Because the acting group is the translations, every core in one combinatorial type has the same count. The surprise — all 65 cores, not merely one orbit, gave the same integer — says there is only one type at p = 223. Then the count is a function of (T, p) alone:

N(p) = ((p-1) / |Stab(T)|) · μ(T)

where μ(T) is the number of missed classes after the worst 13-placement of T, times the number of completing pairs that do not cover those misses. No row loop. The 496,000 is that number at p = 223, and the 0 failures say μ(T) never hit zero.

ESTIMATE. 496,000 = 2^7 · 5^3 · 31. And p − 1 = 222 = 2 · 3 · 37, while 6,216 = 2^3 · 3 · 7 · 37, so 37 | (p−1) also divides the row index. The shared 37 is the fingerprint of a discrete-log period, not of an accident. The extra 31 and the powers of 5 are the part the formula has to produce; they are not in p−1, so they should come from the width of T (the 1/16-zone) and from choosing 2 completions out of a 31-ish residue set.

The leap, which is the wild part: the same tile argument does not need p = 223. It needs only that a 13-class core exists and that T is a single interval (or a single union of intervals) in discrete-log order. That is exactly the class “p with a 13-class core”, and it is implied by p ≡ 15 (mod 16), because then 16 does not divide p−1, the 1/16-zones cannot tile the circle evenly, and every speed is forced to the same rigid shortfall. One shape check would then close every such prime, instead of a gate per prime. Large-prime cost (the ~74,000 CPU-hours) is the thing this tries to delete.

WHY IT MIGHT WORK
The invariance is already measured, not hoped for. A count that is constant on 65 cores and equal to the global check total is what a group action produces and what an enumeration bug usually does not (a bug drifts). Allikvere’s 14- and 15-runner gates are covering search plus binary lifting; the composite obstruction there was the missing polynomial argument. Here the picture supplies a substitute for that polynomial: a tile that is constant on the multiplicative group. If the tile never covers, the gate is a theorem about one subset of Z/(p−1)Z, and the computer is only asked to print the tile.




CHEAP TEST
Under an hour, one machine, no new gate.

1. Take the existing p = 223 certificate. Pick core C0. Multiply all 13 speed classes by g, g^7, g^{37} (three translations). Recompute the improper-lift count for each translate. FACT to check: each equals 496,000, and the set of missed time classes is the translate of the missed set of C0.
2. From C0 alone, export the tile T as a bitset of length 222. Compute N by the formula above (stabiliser order, missed-class count, completing pairs) with no loop over the other 64 cores. FACT to check: N = 496,000 exactly.
3. Repeat step 2 on one already-closed prime (p = 239) and one prime ≡ 15 (mod 16) that is not yet a gate, e.g. 191 or 271: build T once, evaluate N, and compare with a 200-row sample of cores, not the full gate. Agreement on the sample is the signal; full closure is not required for the test.

WHAT KILLS IT
Any one of these.

- Two cores at p = 223 that are not discrete-log translates, yet were both counted, and a fresh translate of C0 does not give 496,000. Then the constant was a property of the enumerator, not of the tile.
- The closed form from T disagrees with 496,000 by any nonzero amount. The formula is wrong; invariance alone does not remove the enumeration.
- At p = 239 or at the first p ≡ 15 (mod 16), two sampled cores give different improper counts. Then “one shape” is special to 223 and does not close a class.
- T is not a bounded union of intervals in discrete-log order (shape entropy grows with p). Then there is nothing to check once, and the picture does not become a proof.
