## Central Question

Can a constructive time formula choose its own phase anchor when a fixed locked pair fails?

For arbitrary real speeds:

0 < a < b < c < d

seek an explicit time t satisfying all four distance constraints at threshold 1/5.

Focus especially on the middle corridor:

4a < d < 4c.

## Track A — Opus · Anchor Switching

Study a finite menu of phase anchors, including:

t = tau/(b+c),\
t = tau/(a+d),\
t = tau/(a+c).

Determine whether an algebraic rule based on the speed ratios can select an anchor and phase tau without exhaustive search.

Extend the proven Half-Lock and C1 constructions.

Deliver one rigorous new sufficient region or a proved obstruction to a proposed selection rule.

## Track B — Qwen · Symbolic Selection

Study whether a finite set of phase choices can cover a continuous region that ordinary integer locks miss.

Investigate rational offsets such as integer and half-integer tau.

A numerical observation of repeating offsets is not proof.

Give exact conditions, not statistical coverage estimates.

## Track C — Argon · Adversarial Anchor Test

Find rigorous counterexamples to proposed adaptive-anchor rules.

Distinguish failure of a fixed anchor from failure of all possible anchors.

Do not extrapolate finite examples into universal impossibility.

## Track D — Independent Reviews

1. The Half-Lock Box lemma HB.
2. The Connected Region lemma C1.

Check the full real-speed domains, exact endpoints, equality and strict inequalities, and branch-selection determinism.

Return the review verdicts separately.

## Cross-Round Question · Toward a General Recurrence

Explore whether the constructive pattern can extend from n runners to n+1 runners.

Use the existing general conditional n-runner lemma and the proven Lock-plus-Nudge mechanism as starting points.

Do not assume success at six, twelve, or any finite number of runners proves a universal recurrence.

The target is a mathematically proved operation, not observed repetition.
